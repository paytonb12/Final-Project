Half-Real

Theory

Jesper Juul discusses how video games consist of real rules working in a fictional world. He states, “To play a video game is therefore to interact with real rules while imagining a fictional world, and a video game is a set of rules as well as a fictional world,” (Juul 1). In other words, Jesper Juul’s concept of “half-real” means that video games are both real and fictional at the same time. Video games are real because they contain rules that players interact with, and winning or losing is a real outcome. At the same time, they are fictional because the characters, stories, and worlds inside the game are fictional and not real (Juul 1).

Connection to Stardew Valley

The game reflects Jesper Juul’s idea of games as “half-real.” It involves real elements like the daytime clock and energy limit, and farming mechanics, with a fictional story about rural life, and forming relationships. Players navigate both the real mechanical systems and the fictional world, making the experience of the game half-real.

Stardew Valley demonstrates the half-real concept with its structured gameplay systems in the fictional rural setting with the community and storyline that shapes the game. Players manage real, rule mechanics like crop growth cycles, resource management, and an in-game day and night clock while also engaging with a narrative about restoring the farm, developing relationships with townspeople and having a connection with the community. This balance between the system rules and fiction story creates an experience that is both strategic and creates an emotional connection illustrating Juul’s concept of the “half-real” nature of video games in the reading, Half-Real: Video Games Between Real and Fictional.
